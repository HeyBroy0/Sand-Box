# Document Review and Assessment

**Purpose:** Assess an existing document before editing, consolidating, preserving, or routing it.

## Review dimensions

1. **Purpose and audience:** What task does the document support, and who should use it?
2. **Ownership:** Which component is responsible for maintaining the operational source?
3. **Authority and status:** Is it a requirement, procedure, recommendation, proposal, example, historical copy, generated output, or navigation artifact?
4. **Accuracy:** Are its claims, paths, interfaces, permissions, and examples supported by the current repository or service?
5. **Usability:** Can the intended reader understand and apply it without loading unrelated material?
6. **Structure:** Are sections, headings, terminology, and references appropriate to its purpose?
7. **Lifecycle:** Is it operational, historical, generated, navigational, or a working draft?
8. **Reachability:** Can the intended reader find it from the relevant entrypoint or component route?

## Review method

Read the document and only the sources needed to establish its owner, status, and important claims. Check important links and references. Separate factual defects from stylistic preferences. Identify duplicated authority or genuine overlap before recommending consolidation.

## Review output

A useful review states:
- What the document is for and who owns it.
- Its current status and authority.
- What is accurate and useful.
- Concrete defects or missing context.
- Suggested changes, with uncertain decisions clearly labeled.

Do not silently rewrite a proposal into policy, infer adoption from a filename, or recommend uniform formatting without a usability reason.
