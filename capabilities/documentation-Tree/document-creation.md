# Document Creation and Editing

**Purpose:** Create or revise documents that are accurate, locally usable, and clearly owned.

## Before writing

1. Define the document's task, purpose, audience, and expected use.
2. Find the nearest sensible operational owner and check for an existing canonical source.
3. Classify the intended document as an operational source, core-native source, historical copy, generated composite, navigation document, or draft.
4. Identify any shared rule that must be linked rather than duplicated.
5. Choose a format that suits the reader and maintenance lifecycle; do not impose a universal template without a demonstrated need.

## Writing requirements

- State purpose and applicability near the beginning.
- Distinguish mandatory requirements from recommendations, rationale, examples, and proposals.
- Use direct language and task-appropriate headings.
- Include enough context, steps, limitations, assumptions, and references for the intended reader to use the document independently.
- Make source ownership and document status explicit when confusion is possible.
- Use repository-relative links for repository paths and verify them.
- Avoid unsupported claims about tools, permissions, tests, controls, or automation.
- Keep scope focused. Split a document only when the parts have distinct readers, purposes, owners, or lifecycles and benefit from independent use.

## After writing

Check factual claims against their sources. Verify links and anchors. Confirm that the document does not create competing authority or point routine readers to historical or generated material. Update the minimum relevant navigation route.

## Special document types

- **Historical copy:** Add provenance and status; do not silently change captured source content.
- **Generated composite:** Declare inputs and generation method; do not edit the output manually.
- **Navigation document:** List concise triggers and destinations rather than copying full instructions.
- **Proposal:** Label it clearly and keep it separate from adopted requirements.

For broad consolidation, source migration, or repository-wide navigation changes, use the relevant Maintenance scope rather than expanding an ordinary document-creation task.
