# Alignment Maintenance

**Status:** Proposed operational guide; not adopted as authority.

**Use when:** Related documents, components, ownership, terminology, or navigation have drifted enough to create real confusion, duplication, broken reachability, or avoidable effort.

## Focus

Restore coherence without turning alignment into broad capability development or architectural redesign.

- Establish the canonical source for shared requirements; link to it rather than maintaining competing copies.
- Consolidate genuine overlap while preserving differences in authority, purpose, audience, scope, and behavior.
- Clarify ownership, status, and lifecycle before moving or merging documents.
- Repair routes and references that prevent readers reaching the operational source.
- Keep local explanations where they make an independently used component understandable.
- Prefer direct links and the smallest navigation structure that solves the problem.

## Working approach

Inspect the affected sources, their owners, and their direct consumers. Make the smallest set of changes that restores coherence. Verify the affected routes and preserve original or historical material where required.

Do not normalize for appearance alone. Do not silently change core policy, architecture, authority, entrypoints, or shared contracts. When alignment reaches those surfaces, stop and use the protected-change route.

Use Minimal Maintenance for a bounded local correction. Use Improvement Maintenance when the problem requires a broader capability or design change.

A successful alignment leaves the area easier to understand and navigate without adding unnecessary process to ordinary local work.
